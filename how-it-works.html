Behind the scenes

How scores, distances and review checks work

Smart score (the “match” shown on stays). A number out of 100 that shows how well a stay fits what you searched for. It mixes guest ratings (the biggest part), how close the stay is, how good the price is compared with other results, and how often guests have stayed there. Higher means a better overall match. It is a guide, never a rule, and you always make the final choice.

Distance on the map. The “km away” you see is the straight-line distance between two places, as a bird would fly. It is worked out from their map positions using the haversine method, which allows for the Earth being round. Roads bend, so a drive is usually longer than the number shown.

Attraction fees. Entry fees shown for parks and sights are only a guide to what you may pay at the gate. They are set by the site owner, often the government, and are never added to your booking. The only extra we can add is a tour guide or attraction that a host offers inside their own property, and it is always shown before you book.

Review checks. New reviews pass a simple checklist that looks for signs of advertising, copied text, bursts of reviews from one person, or ratings far from everyone else’s. It only gives an administrator a hint about what to read first. People, not software, decide what is shown.
